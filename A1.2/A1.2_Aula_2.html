Subject pronouns

Subject pronounWho or what?Example
Ithe speakerI am Akane.
youone or more listenersYou are my friend. / You are my friends.
heone boy or manHe is my brother.
sheone girl or womanShe is my mother.
itone thing or animalIt is my dog.
wethe speaker + other peopleWe are cousins.
theytwo or more people or thingsThey are my grandparents.

Plural pronoun math

I + someone = WE
Sofia + I = We
YOU + someone = YOU
The same form is singular or plural.
HE / SHE / IT + someone = THEY
Rosa + Daniel = They
Math tip: I becomes we when the speaker joins another person. You keeps the same form for one listener or many listeners. Other people or things together become they.

Possessive adjectives

SubjectPossessive adjectiveFamily example
ImyMy brother is Leo.
youyourIs Rosa your mother?
hehisHis wife is Elena.
sheherHer husband is Marco.
weourOur parents are Elena and Marco.
theytheirTheir mother is Rosa.
Important: use her mother, not she mother. Use their children, not they children.

Names + ’s / plural s’

Show who is connected to whom.

12 min
FormExample
one person / name + ’sElena’s brother is Daniel.
name ending in s + ’Lucas’ mother is a teacher.
plural ending in s + ’The parents’ children are Sofia and Leo.
irregular plural + ’sThe children’s grandmother is Rosa.
two names + ’s after the last nameElena and Marco’s children are Sofia and Leo.
Notice: if a name ends in s, use only an apostrophe in this material: Lucas’ mother (not Lucas’s mother). Possessive adjectives do not take an apostrophe: his brother, her mother, their children.
